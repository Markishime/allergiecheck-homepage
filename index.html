Intended use

The right format depends on the right person.

Nasal epinephrine is prescription-only. Suitability is specific to the individual, the product label, and local availability — not a one-size-fits-all choice.

01 /

Recognize the signs

Know the symptoms your clinician has told you to treat. Breathing trouble, swelling, widespread hives, fainting, or severe stomach symptoms can be signs of anaphylaxis.

02 /

Follow the prescribed plan

Use medication only as your product instructions and emergency action plan direct. Call emergency services after use, even if symptoms improve.

03 /

Make support visible

Tell family, friends, school, or coworkers where your plan and medicine are kept — and when they should act.

Use & access

From prescription to being prepared.

Access looks different across health systems. These are useful questions to bring to a clinician or pharmacist.

A /

How is it prescribed?

Ask whether a nasal epinephrine format is available and appropriate for you. The prescriber will use the specific product label and your circumstances to decide.

B /

Where can it be obtained?

Your pharmacy or healthcare team can explain local supply, dispensing, coverage, and any product-specific storage or replacement instructions.

C /

How should it be carried?

Keep prescribed treatment accessible when you are away from home, and check its expiry date and storage requirements on the packaging.

Practical details

Questions for life beyond the appointment.

Away from home

Ask how to keep medication available at school, work, travel, or activities — and who should know your action plan.

Shipping & refills

Delivery timing, returns, and refills depend on the pharmacy, insurer, and country. Confirm these directly before you rely on them.

Regular nasal sprays

An ordinary allergy nasal spray is not an emergency epinephrine treatment. Do not treat the two as interchangeable.

Support

For device training and product questions, use the manufacturer’s instructions and speak with your pharmacist or healthcare team.

Common questions

Answers that make room for nuance.

Helpful context, not personal medical advice. For individual care, talk to a healthcare professional.

Who can use epinephrine nasal spray?+

The intended patient population, weight or age limits, dosing, and instructions are specific to each approved product. A prescriber can determine whether it is appropriate for you or your child.

Can a regular allergy spray replace emergency epinephrine?+

No. Regular allergy sprays are not substitutes for prescribed epinephrine in the treatment of anaphylaxis. Follow your emergency action plan and seek emergency medical help.

How can I get a prescription nasal spray?+

Availability and prescribing rules vary. Speak with a healthcare professional or pharmacist about approved options, product labeling, coverage, and access in your area.

What should I carry for an allergy emergency?+

Carry your prescribed emergency medication and a current action plan as directed by your clinician. Check expiry dates and storage instructions regularly.
